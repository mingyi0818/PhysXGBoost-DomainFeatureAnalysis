Dear Editor,

We submit our manuscript "Building Physics-Derived Feature Augmentation for Tree-Based Appliance Energy Prediction: A Fair Multi-Model Comparison" for consideration in Energy and Buildings.

This work proposes a building physics-derived feature engineering framework that augments raw sensor measurements with features encoding thermal-humidity coupling (THI), air enthalpy, stack effect, wind chill, and spatial temperature heterogeneity. Using 19,735 ten-minute measurements from a residential building with nine-room sensor networks, we demonstrate that physics-derived features consistently improve tree-based energy prediction across XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, and Random Forest (R² improvement: +2.2 to +4.2 percentage points).

Key contributions:
(1) Systematic encoding of building physics principles as derived features for ML energy prediction.
(2) Rigorous multi-model, multi-seed (n=7) comparison with statistical significance testing.
(3) Identification of THI and indoor-outdoor temperature differential as the most impactful physics-derived features.

We believe this work aligns with Energy and Buildings' scope in smart building systems and data-driven energy management.
